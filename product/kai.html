What to ask it

Four jobs, in roughly the order people use them

Campaign intelligence

“Which creative moved the most accounts from Aware to Engaged last month, and what did each movement cost?”

Account briefings

“Everything Ravensbourne Group has seen from us since June, in a paragraph I can read before a call at three.”

Attribution

“What opened in Q2 at accounts we had engaged twice before the opportunity was created?”

Reporting

“Write the quarterly summary for the DACH programme: coverage, movement, spend, and the two things I should change.”

How it stays honest

Four constraints we built in on purpose

  • It shows its query. Every answer expands to the filter and date range it ran. If the answer looks wrong, you can see why in one click.
  • It refuses small samples. Below the confidence threshold Kai says the sample is too small rather than producing a number that will get quoted in a deck.
  • It cannot spend money. Kai proposes budget and audience changes; a human approves them. There is no autopilot that quietly reallocates your quarter.
  • It stays in scope. Kai answers from your workspace data. It is not a general assistant, and it will tell you when a question is outside what it can see.
