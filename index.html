The gap

Campaign Manager was built for demand gen, not for named accounts.

It will happily tell you that a campaign delivered 41,382 impressions at a £58 CPM. It will not tell you that eleven of those impressions landed on the VP of Engineering at an account your AE has been chasing since March — or that the other 380 accounts on your list have not been reached once.

So ABM teams end up exporting CSVs, stitching them to a target list by hand, and presenting a number in the QBR they quietly do not trust. Kairo removes that entire step.

The loop

Five things have to happen in order. Kairo does four of them.

This is a sequence, not a menu — each step depends on the one above it. You still own step five.

Step 01 — Resolve

Turn impressions into companies

Kairo matches ad delivery back to the firmographic record and to your uploaded target list, so every campaign has a company-level view from day one — including the accounts that got nothing.

Step 02 — Watch

Track the buying committee, not the click

One click is noise. Several job functions from the same account engaging inside a few weeks is a signal. Kairo tracks breadth and depth per account, and flags when a new function appears for the first time.

Step 03 — Score

Score the account and move its stage

Breadth, depth, seniority and recency roll into a single score with a stage attached. Weightings are yours to set, and the history is kept so you can see what a score was built from.

Step 04 — Attribute

Tie spend to the pipeline that followed it

Opportunities are matched to accounts, not to last-click sessions. You get influenced pipeline on a touch definition you can defend, and cost per account moved — the number that governs the budget conversation.

Step 05 — Yours

Your rep makes the call

Kairo hands sales a scored, ranked list with the ads each contact saw and when. What happens on the call is still the part software should not pretend to do.

Questions

The things people ask on the first call

Does this need a pixel on my website?
No, though it helps. Company-level ad engagement comes from the ad platforms and Kairo's firmographic matching, so you get an account view without touching your site. Adding the Kairo tag lets us join ad engagement to on-site behaviour for the same account, which improves stage accuracy and makes attribution windows tighter.
How is an “account reached” defined?
One or more impressions delivered to a person Kairo can resolve to that company, inside the reporting window. It is deliberately a low bar — reach is a coverage metric, not a quality metric. Engagement, stage and score are where the quality judgement lives, and all three have thresholds you set yourself.
What happens to accounts LinkedIn cannot match?
They stay on the list, marked unmatched, with the reason. Match rates on a clean target list are usually in the high seventies to low nineties depending on region and company size; anything below that is normally a list-hygiene problem, and Kairo will tell you which rows are failing and why.
Can I use this if most of my spend is not on LinkedIn?
Yes, but be honest about the fit. Kairo is strongest where LinkedIn is the primary account-reach channel, because that is where person-level targeting produces a reliable company signal. Google and Reddit are supported for attribution and appear in the account timeline; they are not where the account graph comes from.
How long before the numbers are worth showing anyone?
The backfill gives you 90 days of history within the first hour, so coverage and cost-per-account-reached are meaningful immediately. Scoring needs about three weeks of live data before stage transitions are stable, and attribution needs at least one full sales cycle before anyone should quote an influenced-pipeline figure in a board deck.
Is our target list used to train anything?
No. Target lists, CRM data and campaign data are yours, are not used to train models, and are not pooled across customers. Aggregate benchmarks are opt-in and are computed from anonymised, minimum-cohort data only.
