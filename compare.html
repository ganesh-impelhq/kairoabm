Where Kairo fits, and where it honestly does not.

There are three broad shapes of tool in this market. They solve genuinely different problems, and the fastest way to be disappointed by any of them is to buy the wrong shape. Here is the honest map.

Ad-side ABM measurement

Kairo sits here. Start from a named list, measure whether your paid programme reached it, score the accounts that engaged, attribute what opened afterwards. Best when LinkedIn is your main account-reach channel.

Website de-anonymisation

Identifies companies visiting your site. Genuinely useful, and a different job: it tells you who arrived, not whether your programme reached the list you chose. Many teams run one of each.

Full-stack ABM suites

Orchestration, ad serving, sales plays and measurement in one contract. The right answer for a large team with a dedicated ABM function and the budget to match. Overkill for most teams under 200 people.

Be careful

Four situations where Kairo is the wrong purchase

Your list is under about 50 accounts

At that size a spreadsheet and a weekly conversation with your AEs genuinely works. Buy software when the manual version has started producing numbers people distrust.

You are not running paid at all

Kairo measures a paid account-reach programme. With no ad spend there is nothing for the account graph to be built from, and you want a website-identification tool instead.

You need the ads served for you

Kairo measures and scores; it does not buy media or serve creative. If you want one vendor to do both, you want a full-stack suite and should budget accordingly.

Your sales team will not act on the output

The honest failure mode. If nobody owns following up on an account that enters In market, better measurement produces a better-documented flat quarter.
